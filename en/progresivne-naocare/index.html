When you need progressive lenses

After 40, the eye gradually loses its ability to focus up close — this is presbyopia, a natural change that happens to everyone. You hold your phone further away, the print gets smaller, and next to your distance glasses you also need reading glasses.

A progressive lens combines it all: the top is for distance, the middle for the computer and conversation, the bottom for reading. The transition is gradual and invisible — no line like bifocals, so they look like ordinary glasses.

How it works

Three zones in one lens

Distance

Top of the lens: driving, the street, TV.

Intermediate

Middle: computer, kitchen, conversation at the table.

Near

Bottom: phone, book, small print.

No line

A smooth transition between zones — it looks like an ordinary lens.

Tips

How to adapt faster

Wear them all day

Put them on in the morning and keep them on. The brain adapts fastest when you don't switch glasses.

Turn your head

To look to the side, turn your head, not just your eyes — that way you look through the clearest part of the lens.

Eyes down to read

To read, lower your eyes, not your head. At the computer, raise your chin slightly until the image is sharp.

Take stairs slowly

In the first days, tilt your head slightly on stairs and look through the top of the lens.
