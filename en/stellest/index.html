Why it matters to slow down myopia

More and more children become myopic, and at a younger age — they spend more time indoors, looking at phones, tablets and books up close. The earlier myopia appears, the faster it progresses.

Myopia of −1.00 D that appears at age 7 is likely to reach about −6.00 D by age 16. High myopia carries a greater risk of serious eye conditions later in life. That is why the goal today is not only clear vision, but also keeping the prescription from growing.

How it works

Two zones in one lens

Sharp vision

The central zone is a single-vision lens with the child's prescription. Children see as clearly as with ordinary glasses.

H.A.L.T. technology

Around the centre, 1,021 invisible lenslets are arranged in 11 rings (Highly Aspherical Lenslet Target).

A signal for the eye

The lenslets create a “volume of signal” in front of the retina that slows down eye elongation — and with it, myopia progression.

Quick adaptation

No special restrictions. All children adapt to Stellest lenses within one week.²

67%

slower myopia progression on average compared with single-vision lenses, when worn 12 hours a day, over 2 years.¹

Who it's for

For every myopic child

  • Children with newly diagnosed myopia and children whose myopia is already progressing.
  • Myopia up to −12.00 D and astigmatism up to 4.00 D (depending on lens diameter).
  • Lenses are made only from an ophthalmologist's prescription, after an eye examination.
  • Airwear® 1.59 material — light, thin and impact-resistant, ideal for active children. Blocks 100% of UV.

Warranty

Stellest warranty

Stellest lenses come with the Essilor Premium warranty. In addition, the Stellest warranty applies: if the child's prescription changes by more than 0.50 D during the first year of wear, the lenses are replaced once with new Stellest lenses free of charge — provided the child wore the glasses at least 12 hours a day, attended all check-ups (after 2 weeks and every 6 months) and the Stellest passport is presented.

Tips for parents

Getting the best results

12 hours a day

The more your child wears Stellest glasses, the more effective the slowdown. Recommended: 12 hours a day, every day.

2 hours outdoors

Whenever possible, let your child spend 2 hours a day outdoors in daylight.

Breaks from close work

Regular breaks during long reading, writing, phone and tablet use.

Regular check-ups

A check-up after 2 weeks and every 6 months — so we can see whether the prescription is stable.
